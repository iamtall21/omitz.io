01 / Hydrogen

NFPA 2: Hydrogen Technologies Code (2023)

A comprehensive review of the core safety requirements for hydrogen production, storage, transfer and use, with the explanatory material needed to apply the code correctly.

8 hours · live, in person

Objectives

  • Explain the role of NFPA 2 in the safe deployment of hydrogen
  • Locate the sections that apply to a given system or facility
  • Interpret key requirements and apply them to real projects

Who should attend

  • Designers, engineers (mechanical, chemical, fire protection), researchers, technicians and project managers who design, install or maintain hydrogen systems
  • Authorities having jurisdiction and safety and health staff responsible for compliance
Course outline
Fundamentals (Chapters 1–5)
Administration, references and definitions; general fire safety, permits, emergency plans and training; the performance-based option and the AHJ’s role in approving it.
General hydrogen requirements (Chapter 6)
Building design and construction, occupancy classification, control areas, piping and storage conditions for gaseous and liquefied hydrogen.
Gaseous hydrogen (Chapters 7 and 10)
System design, installation and control; metal hydride storage; separation distances for bulk and non-bulk systems; hazard area classification; GH2 vehicle fueling facilities.
Liquefied hydrogen (Chapters 8 and 11)
Container and system design, piping and valves, separation distances, shutoff and pressure-relief devices; LH2 fueling facilities.
Fuel cells and generation (Chapters 12–13)
Stationary, portable and vehicle fuel cell power systems; hydrogen generation systems and electrolyzers.
Special applications (Chapters 14–19)
Combustion and special atmosphere applications, laboratory operations, parking and repair garages, and hydrogen-powered industrial trucks.

02 / Energy storage

NFPA 855: Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems (2023)

A comprehensive review of the safety requirements for installing, commissioning, operating, maintaining and decommissioning stationary energy storage systems.

Formats

Overview
2 hours
Fundamentals
4 hours · Chapters 1–8
Advanced
4 hours · Chapters 9–19
Complete
8 hours · all chapters

Who should attend

  • Designers, engineers (electrical, mechanical, fire protection), developers and project managers responsible for energy storage systems
  • Battery manufacturers, system integrators and renewable energy professionals
  • Authorities having jurisdiction, fire marshals, building inspectors and emergency responders
Course outline
Fundamentals (Chapters 1–8)
Administration and definitions; general requirements including construction documents, hazard mitigation analysis, detection and suppression; system interconnections; commissioning; operation and maintenance; decommissioning.
Electrochemical systems (Chapter 9)
UL 9540 listing and UL 9540A fire testing, energy storage management systems, dedicated and non-dedicated locations, size and spacing limits, ventilation, thermal runaway and explosion control, rooftop, garage and mobile installations.
Other technologies (Chapters 11–14)
Fuel cell and flywheel systems, and storage of lithium metal and lithium-ion batteries in warehouses and collection sites.
Residential (Chapter 15)
One- and two-family dwellings and townhouses: permitted locations, energy limits, impact protection and smoke alarms.
Annexes A–H
Battery hazards, firefighting considerations, permits and inspections, and suppression guidance for lithium-ion systems.

03 / Professional practice

Ethical AI Workflow for ESH Professionals

A practical introduction to AI built around the two things ESH professionals need first: a working ethical framework and a complete, validated workflow.

Half day · virtual

What participants do

  • Apply ethical decision-making aligned with the BGC and BCSP codes to AI-driven OEHS scenarios
  • Distinguish appropriate from inappropriate uses of AI in OEHS work
  • Build, validate and stress-test a working micro OEHS tool, such as a hazard banding app

Participants leave with a working tool, a repeatable workflow and an ethics framework they can apply across their practice. No prior AI or programming experience is required. A full-day version was presented at AIHA Connect 2026.

Audience: mid- to late-career OEHS leaders (CIH, CSP or equivalent) who want to lead responsible AI adoption in their organizations.
